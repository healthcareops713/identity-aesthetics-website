The short answer

What is Laser Rejuvenation?

Laser rejuvenation is an umbrella term for energy-based treatments selected to improve pigment, texture or visible photoaging. The exact device, wavelength, depth and settings matter; a consultation should name the technology and the concern it is intended to address.

Concerns it may address

  • Sun damage, uneven tone and benign pigment
  • Fine lines, rough texture or enlarged-looking pores
  • Selected scars or stretch-mark appearance when an appropriate device is available
  • A staged skin-renewal plan with realistic expectations
Candidacy

Who may—or may not—be a candidate

Who may be considered

Candidates are selected by skin type, diagnosis, recent sun exposure, medications, healing history and the available device’s cleared indications. Darker skin can be treated with some technologies, but settings and risk counseling require expertise.

When treatment may not be appropriate

Active infection, open wounds, recent tanning, photosensitizing drugs, pregnancy, uncontrolled disease, certain scar histories or an undiagnosed lesion may postpone or change treatment.

Your visit

What happens during treatment

Evaluate

The provider diagnoses the primary concern, reviews skin type and chooses a device rather than treating every discoloration alike.

Plan & treat

Eye protection and skin preparation are used. Energy is delivered with parameters selected for the target and tissue response.

Review & follow up

Aftercare, sun avoidance and the expected healing pattern are reviewed. A follow-up determines whether settings or timing should change.

Informed choice

Important risks, limitations and alternatives

Risks & limitations

Temporary redness, swelling, dryness or pigment darkening can occur. Burns, blistering, prolonged pigment change, infection and scarring are uncommon but important risks. Results vary with device, settings, skin type, concern and aftercare; treatment does not stop future aging or sun damage.

Alternatives to discuss

  • Medical-grade skincare and daily sun protection
  • Chemical peels or Glo2Facial
  • Microneedling or radiofrequency for selected texture goals
  • Surgical options for laxity that laser cannot meaningfully correct
Clinical judgment

Why provider experience matters

Different pigments and scars do not respond alike. Provider experience reduces the chance of treating the wrong diagnosis, using excessive energy or overlooking skin-cancer warning signs.

Common questions

Laser Rejuvenation FAQ

Which laser is best?
There is no universal best laser. Diagnosis, skin type, goal, downtime tolerance and the device’s cleared indications determine the safest match.
Can laser remove stretch marks?
Some treatments may soften color or texture, but complete removal cannot be promised and multiple sessions may be needed.
Can I tan before treatment?
Recent tanning often increases burn and pigment risk. Follow the practice’s sun-avoidance instructions and disclose all exposure.
