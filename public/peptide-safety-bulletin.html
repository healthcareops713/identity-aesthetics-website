What We Found

Real criminal cases, FDA warnings and documented patient harm

Authorities have documented illegal importation, repackaging, counterfeit products, unsanitary manufacturing, dosing errors and at least one related online-injectable contamination case with significant, potentially life-threatening injury.

What We Did Not Find

No verified government finding of a death caused by the named wellness peptides

We did not find an authoritative U.S. government case proving death or permanent disability directly caused by BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295 or ipamorelin. That absence does not establish safety; it means the public human evidence is incomplete and claims must remain precise.

Research-Only Peptides

Unknown does not mean harmless

BPC-157 · FDA evaluation

Three adverse-event reports, major evidence gaps

FDA’s July 2026 review identified reports including injection-site redness and swelling, shortness of breath prompting an emergency-room visit, and diffuse skin and gum darkening after a BPC-157/TB-500 combination. The reports cannot establish causation. FDA also cited insufficient long-term safety information and concerns about impurities, aggregation and immunogenicity.

Read FDA evaluation
TB-500 · FDA evaluation

No adequate human safety record

FDA found no human clinical exposure data or human adverse-event case reports for TB-500 in its review. FDA described theoretical immune risks that can be severe, but this is not proof that such an event has occurred in a patient using TB-500.

Read FDA evaluation
“Research use only” is not a safety certification.

FDA warning letters issued March 31, 2026 said that research disclaimers did not overcome evidence that retatrutide, tirzepatide and related products were being marketed for human use. FDA warned that injectable products can create serious and life-threatening risk when quality is not assured.

Regulatory Watch · July 2026

FDA committee review moved several popular peptides toward exclusion from 503A compounding

FDA’s Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee materials proposed not adding BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, emideltide, Epitalon and Semax—free base and acetate forms—to the 503A bulk drug substances list. A proposal or advisory vote is not the same as a final FDA determination; this bulletin will track the final agency action.

Protect Yourself

If a vial or seller raises concern

  1. Do not inject or discard it.Quarantine the product and preserve the vial, packaging, receipt, shipping container, messages and photographs.
  2. Verify the prescriber and pharmacy independently.Use the applicable state licensing board and FDA outsourcing-facility records—not a badge displayed by the seller.
  3. Seek urgent medical care for serious symptoms.Call 911 for breathing difficulty, facial swelling, fainting, confusion, chest pain, severe abdominal pain or signs of sepsis. In the U.S., Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222.
  4. Report the event and product.Submit adverse events or product-quality problems through FDA MedWatch. Suspected counterfeit or illegal drug sales may also be reported to FDA and law enforcement.
