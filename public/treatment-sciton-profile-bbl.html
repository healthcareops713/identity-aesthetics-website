Technology selected by target

Not one treatment—a customizable platform

Sciton’s platform can host different modules. BBL uses filtered broadband light to target selected pigment and vascular color. ProFractional and Contour TRL use 2940 nm erbium:YAG energy for fractional or full-field resurfacing. A 1064 nm vascular module may address deeper visible vessels when available.

Your consultation must confirm the exact handpiece and module available at Identity Aesthetics. A platform name alone does not establish that every Sciton treatment is appropriate—or installed.
Primary treatment families

Choose the pathway that matches the problem

BBL and erbium resurfacing are not interchangeable. The right path depends on whether the main target is color, vascularity or structure.

Color correction

BBL® BroadBand Light

Interchangeable filters narrow the emitted light to selected wavelength bands. Depending on the filter, settings and diagnosis, BBL may target brown sun damage, freckles, age spots, diffuse redness, visible capillaries, rosacea-like redness or unwanted hair.

Pigmented lesions should be diagnosed before treatment. BBL does not replace a skin-cancer examination.

Texture remodeling

ProFractional® & Contour TRL

ProFractional creates microscopic ablation channels while leaving surrounding skin intact. Contour TRL provides tunable full-field resurfacing. These pathways may improve wrinkles, rough texture, acne or surgical scars and selected stretch-mark appearance, with recovery increasing as treatment depth increases.

Choose by concern

Your most useful starting point

Brown spots & sun damage

Often LaseMD or filter-selected BBL, depending on depth, skin type and diagnosis.

Redness & visible vessels

Usually a Sciton light or vascular pathway—not LaseMD—after identifying the vascular target.

Texture & fine lines

LaseMD for gentler renewal; ProFractional or deeper erbium resurfacing when more remodeling and downtime are acceptable.

Acne or surgical scars

Sciton fractional erbium resurfacing may be considered. Scar type, color and depth determine the plan.

Stretch-mark appearance

Fractional resurfacing may soften texture or contrast. Complete removal cannot be promised and a series is common.

Skin tightening

Collagen remodeling may improve the appearance of mild laxity, but laser is not equivalent to surgical lifting.

Important: BBL is BroadBand Light, not a laser. “Laser rejuvenation” is used here as a consumer-friendly category, while your treatment plan identifies the actual technology.

Filter-guided BBL

Why filters matter for sun damage and redness

BBL uses a range of wavelengths rather than one laser wavelength. The provider selects a filter and pulse settings designed around the intended chromophore—melanin for brown lesions or hemoglobin for vascular redness—then adjusts for skin type and target depth. This flexibility is why BBL can be compelling for mixed visible photoaging, but it also makes operator judgment essential.

Brown pigment

Sun spots, freckles and age-related benign pigment may temporarily darken before gradually flaking or fading.

Redness & vessels

Selected filters can target diffuse redness, small visible vessels and cherry angiomas when clinically appropriate.

Combination plans

BBL may be paired or staged with resurfacing when both color and texture are priorities. Combining treatments can also increase recovery and risk.

Special concern pathways

Scars, stretch marks, tightening and toenails

These concerns require more diagnostic precision than a standard “facial rejuvenation” visit. Scar type, stretch-mark age and color, degree of laxity and the cause of a nail change all affect whether energy treatment is reasonable.

  • Scars: ProFractional or deeper erbium resurfacing may soften texture; complete erasure is not realistic.
  • Stretch marks: Fractional resurfacing may reduce visible texture or contrast, often over a series.
  • Mild laxity: Collagen remodeling may improve appearance, but it cannot reproduce a surgical lift.
  • Toenail fungus: Nail changes should be confirmed as fungal before treatment. A 1064 nm laser protocol may be discussed only when the appropriate module is available; results vary, recurrence is possible and standard antifungal care may still be needed.
The short answer

What is the Sciton JOULE / Profile platform?

It is a modular medical laser and light platform. The attached treatment module—not the console name—determines the energy delivered, the target and the recovery. Identity Aesthetics uses this flexibility to build concern-specific plans rather than treating every patient with the same setting.

Concerns it may address

  • Sun damage, freckles, age spots and uneven-looking tone
  • Diffuse redness, selected visible vessels and vascular discoloration
  • Fine lines, wrinkles and rough texture
  • Acne scars, surgical scars and selected stretch-mark appearance
Candidacy

Who may—or may not—be a candidate

Who may be considered

Candidates are selected by skin type, diagnosis, recent sun exposure, medications, healing history and the available device’s cleared indications. Darker skin can be treated with some technologies, but settings and risk counseling require expertise.

When treatment may not be appropriate

Active infection, open wounds, recent tanning, photosensitizing drugs, pregnancy, uncontrolled disease, certain scar histories or an undiagnosed lesion may postpone or change treatment.

Your visit

What happens during treatment

Evaluate

The provider diagnoses the primary concern, reviews skin type and chooses a device rather than treating every discoloration alike.

Plan & treat

Eye protection and skin preparation are used. Energy is delivered with parameters selected for the target and tissue response.

Review & follow up

Aftercare, sun avoidance and the expected healing pattern are reviewed. A follow-up determines whether settings or timing should change.

Informed choice

Important risks, limitations and alternatives

Risks & limitations

Temporary redness, swelling, dryness or pigment darkening can occur. Burns, blistering, prolonged pigment change, infection and scarring are uncommon but important risks. Results vary with device, settings, skin type, concern and aftercare; treatment does not stop future aging or sun damage.

Alternatives to discuss

  • Medical-grade skincare and daily sun protection
  • Chemical peels or Glo2Facial
  • Microneedling or radiofrequency for selected texture goals
  • Surgical options for laxity that laser cannot meaningfully correct
Clinical judgment

Why provider experience matters

Different pigments and scars do not respond alike. Provider experience reduces the chance of treating the wrong diagnosis, using excessive energy or overlooking skin-cancer warning signs.

Common questions

Sciton, BBL & resurfacing FAQ

Is BBL a laser?
No. BBL is filtered broadband light. Sciton also offers true laser modules, including erbium:YAG resurfacing and 1064 nm vascular technology.
Will one treatment fix all sun damage?
No guarantee can be made. Some people notice visible change after an early treatment, while others need a series and ongoing sun protection. New sun damage can still develop.
Can Sciton remove stretch marks or scars?
Fractional or deeper erbium resurfacing may soften visible texture and contrast, but complete removal is not realistic. Scar type, skin type and healing history affect candidacy.
Does laser cure toenail fungus?
A nail diagnosis comes first because trauma, psoriasis and other conditions can resemble fungus. When an appropriate 1064 nm protocol is available, it may be discussed as one option, but clearance of the nail and prevention of recurrence are not guaranteed.
Can I tan before BBL or resurfacing?
Recent tanning may increase burn and pigment risk. Follow the practice’s sun-avoidance instructions and disclose natural, spray and self-tanning exposure.
