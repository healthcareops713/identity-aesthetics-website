Peptides for Real Life

What is a peptide—in plain English?

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids. Your body uses many peptides as messengers, telling cells when to do things such as release insulin, regulate appetite or carry out other specific functions. A peptide-based medicine is designed to act on a defined biological pathway. The right question is not “Which peptide is trending?” It is “Is there a well-supported medicine for my diagnosis, and is it safe for me?”

SignalA peptide can carry a message to a receptor.
ResponseThe body may change a specific process, such as appetite signaling.
SupervisionYour response and adverse effects require clinical follow-up.
Three Very Different Buckets

“Peptide” does not automatically mean proven or appropriate.

We discuss product status openly before a treatment decision.

FDA-approved

Prescription peptide medicines

Approved for specific products, doses, routes and medical uses after FDA review. Approval never means that every person—or every advertised use—is appropriate.

Review approved therapies
Different standard

Compounded medication

Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. They may be considered only when an individualized medical need cannot be met by an available FDA-approved product and applicable law permits.

See our sourcing standards
Not for casual use

Investigational or research-only

A vial labeled “research use only” is not a patient treatment. Retatrutide remains investigational; the FDA says it cannot be used in compounding under federal law.

What Strong Evidence Looks Like

Large trials. Defined patients. Measured outcomes.

Trial averages are not promises; individual results, tolerability and eligibility vary.

14.9%

Semaglutide at 68 weeks

Mean weight change in STEP 1 versus 2.4% with placebo, with lifestyle intervention, in adults with overweight or obesity without diabetes. Read the trial.

15.0–20.9%

Tirzepatide at 72 weeks

Mean weight change across 5, 10 and 15 mg groups versus 3.1% with placebo in SURMOUNT-1. Gastrointestinal effects were most common. Read the trial.

Safety Before Sales

Some online peptide offers are a red flag.

Do not inject products labeled “research use only” or buy prescription drugs from an unverified source. FDA has reported dosing errors and adverse events with unapproved compounded GLP-1 products.

  • No diagnosis or clinician evaluation
  • No current medication review
  • No explanation of FDA or compounding status
  • No licensed pharmacy information
  • No follow-up or adverse-effect plan
