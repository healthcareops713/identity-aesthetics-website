The short answer

What is LaseMD?

LaseMD is a non-ablative thulium laser used in dermatologic procedures. Its cleared indications include soft-tissue coagulation, actinic keratoses and benign pigmented lesions such as age spots, sun spots and freckles. Cosmetic plans are tailored to skin type and concern.

Concerns it may address

  • Sun spots, freckles and uneven pigment
  • Dull tone and rough texture
  • Fine lines and superficial signs of photoaging
  • Selected actinic keratoses when diagnosed and treated under appropriate medical direction
1927 nm thulium technology

A precise path for visible sun damage

LaseMD creates microscopic, non-ablative treatment zones in the skin. The treatment is most directly supported for benign pigmented lesions—including age spots, sun spots and freckles—and for dermatologic procedures requiring soft-tissue coagulation. Settings, treatment density and the number of passes are selected for your skin type, diagnosis and acceptable recovery window.

What it is not: LaseMD is not the answer to every scar, vascular concern or degree of skin laxity. When deeper remodeling, visible vessels or stronger resurfacing is the priority, the Sciton pathway may be more appropriate.

Personalized treatment enhancement

LaseMD ampoule menu

After the laser creates controlled microchannels, your provider may select a topical ampoule based on your skin goals. The pictured product menu includes four options; selection does not guarantee a result and is not a substitute for diagnosis or a home-care plan.

VA

Retinol

Often selected for visible photoaging, surface renewal and uneven-looking tone. Retinoid sensitivity, pregnancy status and current skincare must be reviewed.

RS

Resveratrol

An antioxidant-focused option used in plans for dullness, visible environmental stress and uneven-looking tone.

TA

Tranexamic acid

A pigment-focused option that may be considered for melasma or lentigines. Melasma requires caution because heat and inflammation can worsen it.

VC

Vitamin C

An antioxidant and brightening option for a more even-looking, luminous complexion. “Brightening” means improving visible dullness—not changing your natural skin color.

Candidacy

Who may—or may not—be a candidate

Who may be considered

Adults with compatible skin type and pigment or texture goals may be candidates after assessment. Recent tanning, skin sensitivity, melasma history, medications and tendency toward pigment change affect settings and timing.

When treatment may not be appropriate

Treatment may be deferred for active infection, open skin, recent significant sun exposure or tanning, photosensitizing medicines, pregnancy, uncontrolled medical conditions or a history suggesting excessive scarring or pigment complications.

Informed choice

Important risks, limitations and alternatives

Risks & limitations

Temporary redness, swelling, dryness or pigment darkening can occur. Burns, blistering, prolonged pigment change, infection and scarring are uncommon but important risks. Results vary with device, settings, skin type, concern and aftercare; treatment does not stop future aging or sun damage.

Alternatives to discuss

  • Chemical peel or medical-grade skincare
  • Glo2Facial for a gentler, no-laser experience
  • Sciton BBL or another appropriate pigment device
  • Ablative or fractional resurfacing for deeper concerns, with different downtime and risk
Clinical judgment

Why provider experience matters

Laser safety is more than choosing a setting. Experienced providers identify melasma and lesion patterns, account for skin type and recent exposure, select conservative parameters and manage pigment or burn complications early.

Common questions

LaseMD FAQ

Is LaseMD ablative?
LaseMD is described as a non-ablative thulium laser. It creates controlled treatment zones without the recovery profile of fully ablative resurfacing.
Can it treat melasma?
Melasma can be unpredictable and can worsen with heat or inflammation. It requires diagnosis and a cautious, often multimodal plan.
Do I need sun protection?
Yes. Avoiding tanning and following broad-spectrum sun protection are essential before and after treatment.
