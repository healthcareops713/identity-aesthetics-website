The short answer

What hair restoration involves here

Two non-surgical options are offered, and they work differently. Keralase uses the LaseMD laser to create microchannels in the scalp so KeraFactor—a blend of growth factors and skin proteins—can be absorbed. PRP draws a small amount of your blood, processes it to concentrate the platelets, and injects that back into targeted areas of the scalp.

Both aim to support scalp health and encourage regrowth rather than replace hair that is already gone. Neither is surgery, and neither guarantees a result.

Concerns it may address

  • Early thinning where follicles are still active
  • Reduced density at the crown or part line
  • Thinning associated with stress or hormonal change
  • Scalp condition and circulation alongside density
Two approaches

Keralase and PRP

Which is appropriate depends on your pattern of thinning, your health history and what you want to prioritise.

Keralase — $699 per treatment

The LaseMD laser creates microchannels in the scalp, then KeraFactor serum is applied so it can be absorbed. The aim is to improve scalp health and stimulate dormant follicles. It is generally well tolerated with minimal downtime, and patients commonly notice changes in thickness and scalp condition across a series of sessions.

PRP — platelet-rich plasma

A small blood draw is processed to isolate platelets and their growth factors, which are then injected into targeted scalp areas. It is most often considered for early thinning, where the goal is to strengthen existing hair and support density. Mild discomfort during injection is common and can usually be reduced with a numbing agent.

Your visit

How a course of treatment runs

Evaluate

Your provider reviews health history, medications, thyroid and hormonal factors, prior treatments and the pattern of thinning. Photographs may be taken so change can be judged objectively.

Plan & treat

You receive a recommendation—Keralase, PRP, both, or neither—with pricing and the number of sessions discussed before anything is scheduled.

Review

Progress is reassessed after a defined interval rather than session by session, because visible change takes months.

Informed choice

Risks, limitations and alternatives

Risks & limitations

Expected effects can include temporary scalp redness, tenderness, mild swelling or, with PRP, bruising and brief discomfort at injection sites. Results vary between individuals and are not guaranteed; hair that is no longer growing from a follicle will not be restored by either treatment. PRP is prepared from your own blood, so a blood draw is required. Treatment may be deferred for active scalp infection, certain blood or clotting disorders, some medications, pregnancy or breastfeeding, and other individual factors. Tell your provider about anticoagulants and supplements before treatment.

Alternatives to discuss

  • No treatment, with monitoring over time
  • Evaluation for an underlying medical cause of hair loss, including thyroid and iron studies
  • Topical or oral medical therapy, discussed with an appropriate prescriber
  • Surgical hair transplantation, which is not performed at this practice
  • Hormone evaluation where a hormonal contribution is suspected
Common questions

Hair Restoration FAQ

What is the difference between Keralase and PRP hair restoration?

Keralase uses laser microchannels to deliver KeraFactor growth factors into the scalp. PRP uses platelets concentrated from your own blood. Both aim to support natural regrowth, by different routes, and your provider will discuss which suits your situation.

Are Keralase and PRP treatments painful?

Both are generally well tolerated. Keralase is usually close to painless. PRP may cause mild discomfort during injection, which can often be reduced with a numbing agent.

How many treatments will I need?

Most people are advised a series of 4–6 sessions spaced a few weeks apart. Your provider builds a plan around your goals and the pattern of thinning rather than applying a fixed package.

Who is a good candidate?

These treatments are generally considered for early thinning, or thinning associated with stress or hormonal change, where follicles are still active. A consultation determines whether either option is appropriate and whether an underlying cause should be investigated first.

When will I start seeing results?

Changes in thickness and scalp condition are commonly noticed around 2–3 months, with further change after that. Results vary and are not guaranteed.

Is there any downtime?

Both are minimally invasive and most people return to their usual routine the same day.
