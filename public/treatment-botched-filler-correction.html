The short answer

What is Botched Filler Correction & Repair?

Filler correction begins with diagnosis—not automatic dissolving. The provider evaluates the product used, injection timing, anatomy, symptoms, photographs and whether the concern is excess volume, migration, edema, nodules, infection, asymmetry or a time-sensitive vascular complication.

Concerns it may address

  • Overfilled or distorted facial proportions
  • Suspected filler migration or persistent swelling
  • Lumps, nodules, asymmetry or dissatisfaction after prior filler
  • Planning a safer rebuild after appropriate correction
  • Urgent symptoms after injection, which require immediate assessment
Candidacy

Who may—or may not—be a candidate

Who may be considered

People with a stable, non-emergency concern after prior filler may be candidates for observation, hyaluronidase when suitable, medical treatment, referral or a staged correction plan. Prior records and exact product information are valuable.

When treatment may not be appropriate

Do not wait for a routine appointment if there is severe or increasing pain, pale/gray/dusky skin, coolness, vision change, severe headache, weakness or stroke-like symptoms. Those symptoms require urgent medical evaluation. Hyaluronidase is not appropriate for every filler or problem and can cause allergic reactions.

Your visit

What happens during treatment

Evaluate

The provider reconstructs what was injected, where, when and how symptoms evolved. Photos and records are reviewed when available.

Plan & treat

A careful examination distinguishes aesthetic dissatisfaction from infection, delayed inflammation, nodules or vascular injury.

Review & follow up

Options may include observation, hyaluronidase for selected HA filler, medical treatment, ultrasound-guided care or referral. Re-filling is delayed until tissues can be judged safely.

Informed choice

Important risks, limitations and alternatives

Risks & limitations

Dissolving can remove wanted and unwanted HA, produce unevenness, swelling or bruising, and rarely cause allergic reaction. Correction cannot guarantee restoration of the pre-filler appearance. Non-HA products, scar tissue, infection and vascular injury require different management.

Alternatives to discuss

  • Observation when safe because swelling can mimic overfilling
  • Treatment by the original injector if appropriate and trusted
  • Diagnostic ultrasound or specialist referral
  • Surgical consultation for complications that are not safely managed with office injections

Access & cost

In-office versus telehealth

Evaluation and any corrective procedure are in-office. Telehealth can help collect history or triage, but urgent symptoms need immediate local care and definitive assessment requires examination.

Pricing: Consultation required. Fees depend on diagnostic complexity, product type, number of visits and whether referral or imaging is needed.

Common questions

Botched Filler Correction & Repair FAQ

Can all filler be dissolved?
No. Hyaluronidase acts on hyaluronic-acid filler, not products such as calcium hydroxylapatite or poly-L-lactic acid. Even HA correction may require more than one session.
When can I refill?
Only after inflammation and swelling settle and the provider can reassess anatomy. Rushing to refill can obscure the problem or recreate imbalance.
Is this an emergency?
Vision changes, severe or worsening pain, skin blanching or discoloration, weakness, trouble speaking or severe headache require immediate emergency evaluation.
