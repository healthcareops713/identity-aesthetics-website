The short answer

What is Laser Hair Removal?

Laser hair removal uses light absorbed by pigment in the hair to damage follicles in an active growth phase. It produces long-term hair reduction—not a guarantee that every hair is permanently removed—and usually requires a series because follicles cycle at different times.

Concerns it may address

  • Unwanted dark hair on the face or body
  • Frequent shaving, waxing or related irritation
  • Ingrown-hair tendency after appropriate evaluation
  • Maintenance reduction after an initial series
Candidacy

Who may—or may not—be a candidate

Who may be considered

People with hair containing enough pigment to absorb the selected wavelength may respond. Skin tone, hair color, hormones, medications, tanning and treatment area all affect candidacy and settings.

When treatment may not be appropriate

Treatment may be postponed for tanning, sunburn, active infection, photosensitizing medications or uncontrolled skin conditions. White, gray, very blond or some red hair may respond poorly because it contains little target pigment.

Your visit

What happens during treatment

Evaluate

Hair and skin are assessed; recent waxing, tanning and medication use are reviewed.

Plan & treat

The area is shaved as directed, eye protection is placed and a test spot may be used. Laser pulses are delivered with cooling.

Review & follow up

Treated hairs shed gradually. Sun protection and session timing are important; waxing between visits can disrupt the target.

Informed choice

Important risks, limitations and alternatives

Risks & limitations

Redness, swelling and temporary discomfort are common. Burns, blistering, pigment lightening or darkening, paradoxical hair growth and scarring are uncommon but possible. Results are usually limited for low-pigment hair.

Alternatives to discuss

  • Shaving or depilatory products
  • Waxing, threading or sugaring
  • Electrolysis, including for low-pigment hair
  • Medical evaluation when new or excessive hair growth may reflect a hormonal condition
Clinical judgment

Why provider experience matters

Safe treatment requires matching wavelength, fluence, pulse duration and cooling to skin and hair. Experience is especially important for recently tanned or richly pigmented skin and for recognizing when a hormonal evaluation is warranted.

Common questions

Laser Hair Removal FAQ

Is the hair gone forever?
The goal is long-term reduction. Some follicles recover or new hair develops, especially with hormonal change, so maintenance can be needed.
Can all skin tones be treated?
Many can, with the right device and parameters. The safety margin varies, so skin type and sun exposure must be assessed.
Should I wax before treatment?
Usually no. The follicle target needs to be present. Follow the practice’s preparation instructions for shaving and stopping waxing or plucking.
