The short answer

What is PDO Threads?

PDO thread treatment places absorbable polydioxanone sutures beneath the skin to provide mechanical support and a wound-healing response. Cosmetic thread lifting uses device-specific techniques; the degree and duration of lift are limited compared with surgery.

Concerns it may address

  • Mild lower-face or jawline laxity
  • Early jowling in carefully selected adults
  • Patients seeking a limited, temporary change without facelift surgery
  • Collagen-support goals when expectations are realistic
Candidacy

Who may—or may not—be a candidate

Who may be considered

Best candidates generally have mild-to-moderate laxity, healthy tissue and realistic expectations about a subtle, temporary result. Skin quality, facial weight, prior surgery, filler and asymmetry affect candidacy.

When treatment may not be appropriate

Treatment may be unsuitable with active infection, uncontrolled bleeding risk, poor wound healing, certain autoimmune or connective-tissue disorders, pregnancy or breastfeeding, unrealistic expectations, or laxity that requires surgery.

Your visit

What happens during treatment

Evaluate

The provider assesses tissue quality, vectors, asymmetry and whether threads can realistically meet the goal.

Plan & treat

After antiseptic preparation and local anesthetic, threads are placed through small entry points in planned tissue planes.

Review & follow up

Support is adjusted conservatively. You receive restrictions designed to reduce displacement and instructions for warning signs.

Informed choice

Important risks, limitations and alternatives

Risks & limitations

Pain, swelling, bruising, dimpling, asymmetry, palpable or visible threads, extrusion, infection, nerve or vessel injury, scarring and an unsatisfactory result can occur. Threads may complicate later procedures. A facelift provides a different degree of correction.

Alternatives to discuss

  • No treatment or collagen-support skincare
  • Filler or biostimulator when volume or tissue support—not lifting—is the concern
  • Energy-based tightening for selected mild laxity
  • Surgical facelift or neck lift for more substantial or durable correction
Clinical judgment

Why provider experience matters

Vector planning and tissue-plane control determine both effect and complication risk. A skilled provider can identify when threads are unlikely to deliver value and when surgery or another modality is more appropriate.

Common questions

PDO Threads FAQ

Are PDO threads the same as a facelift?
No. Threads may provide limited temporary support in selected patients; surgery repositions and removes tissue more substantially.
Will I see the threads?
Temporary puckering or palpability can occur. Visible, exposed or persistently painful threads should be evaluated.
Can I combine threads with filler?
Sometimes, but sequencing matters. The provider determines whether combining or staging procedures is safer.
