The short answer

What is professional waxing?

Waxing removes visible hair from the root by applying a professional wax selected for the body area, hair texture and skin condition. It can leave skin feeling smoother for longer than shaving, but it does not permanently stop hair growth. Technique, temperature, section size, skin support and aftercare all matter.

Why clients choose it

  • Smoother skin without daily shaving
  • Defined brows and cleaner facial contours
  • Customized bikini or Brazilian grooming
  • More complete coverage for legs, arms, torso or back
  • A coordinated full-body maintenance plan

Candidacy

Who may—or may not—be a candidate

Who may be considered

Adults seeking temporary hair removal on healthy, intact skin may be candidates. Hair must be long enough for wax to grip, and the area should be free of active sunburn, infection and significant inflammation.

When waxing may be postponed

Waxing should not be performed over broken, blistered, sunburned or actively infected skin. Recent aggressive peels, laser resurfacing, uncontrolled dermatitis, impaired healing or significant irritation may also require postponement. Tell us about diabetes, bleeding risk, skin disorders, allergies and every prescription or topical product used on the area.

Isotretinoin: U.S. labeling instructs patients to avoid wax epilation during treatment and for at least six months afterward because of scarring risk.

Your visit

What happens during professional waxing

Assess & define

Your esthetician reviews medications, recent treatments, sun exposure and skin condition, then confirms the exact areas, borders, privacy preferences and price.

Prepare & remove

Skin is cleansed and prepared. Wax type, temperature and section size are selected for the area; skin is supported as hair is removed in controlled sections.

Calm & protect

Residual wax is removed, the skin is calmed with compatible finishing products and you receive area-specific instructions for friction, heat, exfoliation and rebooking.

Before & after

A little preparation protects the finish

Let hair grow

Hair needs enough visible length for wax to grip. Avoid shaving or tweezing immediately beforehand; ask us about ideal timing for your area and growth pattern.

Arrive clean and dry

When possible, skip heavy lotions, oils and deodorant on the treatment area. Wear clean, loose clothing for larger or intimate-area appointments.

Disclose products and procedures

Tell us about isotretinoin, topical retinoids, exfoliating acids, steroids, blood thinners, recent peels, laser treatments and any new rash or irritation. Do not stop prescribed medication unless the prescribing clinician directs you.

Protect freshly waxed skin

Follow your esthetician’s area-specific instructions. You may be advised to avoid friction, vigorous exercise, hot tubs, pools, heat, fragrance and exfoliation for a short period and to use sun protection on exposed skin.

Informed choice

Risks, limitations and when to call

Possible effects

Temporary redness, tenderness, swelling, follicular bumps, bruising, ingrown hairs, irritation, skin lifting, burns, pigment change, allergic reaction and infection are possible. Results are temporary and hair does not always regrow at the same rate.

Contact us promptly for blistering, severe or worsening pain, spreading redness, drainage, fever or a reaction that does not begin to settle as expected.

About numbing products

Do not self-apply high-strength numbing creams before waxing unless a qualified clinician specifically directs you. The FDA has warned companies marketing certain unapproved topical anesthetic products for cosmetic procedures, including waxing, because excessive absorption can cause serious harm—especially when used over large, irritated or broken areas.

Your esthetician can explain comfort strategies that do not add avoidable medication risk.

Why experience matters

Skin deserves more than a fast strip

Professional waxing is a sequence of judgment calls: whether skin is safe to treat, which wax is appropriate, how warm it should be, how large each section should be and how firmly the skin must be supported. An experienced esthetician also recognizes when medication, irritation or a recent procedure makes postponement the smarter choice.

Common questions

Waxing FAQ

What areas can be waxed?
Common options include brows and facial areas, underarms, arms, legs, bikini line or Brazilian, chest, abdomen, back, shoulders, buttocks, hands, feet and customized combinations. Exact availability is confirmed when booking.
How long should hair be before waxing?
Hair needs enough visible length for wax to grip. Avoid shaving or tweezing immediately before the appointment and ask your esthetician for timing guidance based on the area and your growth cycle.
How long do results last?
Because waxing removes hair from the root, smoothness generally lasts longer than shaving. The interval varies by body area, individual growth cycle and whether all hairs were in the same growth stage.
Can I wax while taking isotretinoin?
No. U.S. isotretinoin labeling says to avoid wax epilation during treatment and for at least six months afterward because of scarring risk. Disclose all oral and topical medications before booking.
Can I wax if I use retinol or exfoliating acids?
Possibly, but not without review. Topical retinoids, strong acids and recent resurfacing can make skin more vulnerable. Tell us the exact product, strength, area and timing; do not stop a prescription unless its prescriber directs you.
Can full-body waxing be done in one appointment?
Sometimes. The safest and most comfortable plan depends on the areas selected, skin condition and appointment length. A customized plan may use one extended visit or staged appointments.
Is waxing available by telehealth?
No. Waxing is an in-office service. You may, however, book online and contact the team in advance with questions about preparation.
