The short answer

Massage at Identity Aesthetics

Sessions are booked at our Fulshear and Katy locations. Each begins with a short consultation so pressure and technique match what you actually want from the session, whether that is tension relief, recovery after activity, or rest.

Massage here is a wellness service rather than a medical treatment, and it is not a substitute for care from a physician or physical therapist for an injury.

Commonly booked for

  • General tension and stress
  • Muscle soreness after activity or long periods at a desk
  • Stiffness affecting range of movement
  • Discomfort in pregnancy, with prenatal technique
  • Rest and recovery, without a specific complaint
Choose a style

Types offered

Not sure which fits? Your therapist will ask what you want from the session and recommend accordingly.

Swedish

Gentle, flowing strokes. Usually chosen for general relaxation and stress relief.

Deep tissue

Firmer pressure into deeper muscle layers, for persistent tightness and areas of chronic stiffness.

Hot stone

Heated stones used alongside massage to warm the tissue and ease tension.

Prenatal

Adapted positioning and technique for pregnancy, commonly for back, hip and ankle discomfort. Let us know your stage of pregnancy when booking.

Sports

Focused on the demands of training—recovery between sessions and areas under repeated load.

Your visit

What a session looks like

Talk first

A short consultation covers what you want from the session, any areas to focus on or avoid, and anything in your health history that matters.

The session

You rest on a massage table, appropriately draped throughout, while your therapist works at a pressure agreed with you. Say so at any point if you want it changed.

Afterwards

Aftercare is simple: drink water, stretch gently, and go easy on strenuous activity for the rest of the day.

Informed choice

Risks, limitations and alternatives

Risks & limitations

Massage is generally safe and well tolerated. Temporary soreness for a day or so afterwards is common, particularly after deep tissue work. Speak to your doctor before booking if you have had recent surgery, a significant injury, a blood clot or clotting disorder, uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, active infection or skin condition in the area, or any pregnancy complication. Tell your therapist about anticoagulants. Massage does not diagnose or treat injury; persistent or worsening pain deserves medical assessment.

Alternatives to discuss

  • Assessment by a physician or physical therapist where there is a specific injury
  • A shorter or gentler session if a full-pressure massage is not appropriate
  • Facials and skin treatments if the goal is primarily rest and skin health
  • Rescheduling if you are unwell on the day
Common questions

Therapeutic Massage FAQ

What should I expect during my massage session?

Every session starts with a short consultation so the work matches what you want. You rest on a massage table, appropriately draped, and your therapist adjusts pressure as you go. Speak up at any point—that is expected, not an interruption.

Who benefits from massage?

People looking for stress relief, help with muscle tightness, support for recovery after training, or simply rest. It is a wellness service, not a treatment for a diagnosed injury.

How do I choose the right type?

Your therapist will ask what you want from the session and recommend. Broadly: Swedish for relaxation, deep tissue or sports for persistent tightness and training recovery, prenatal during pregnancy.

How often should I book?

Monthly suits most people booking for general relaxation. If you are working on a specific area of tightness, more frequent sessions for a period may make sense. Your therapist can advise.

Are there reasons I should not book?

Yes. Check with your doctor first if you have had recent surgery, a significant injury, a clotting disorder or blood clot, uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, an active infection or skin condition in the area, or any complication in pregnancy.

What should I do afterwards?

Drink water, stretch gently, and avoid strenuous activity for a few hours. Mild soreness the next day is common, especially after deep tissue work.
