Verification before medication

Know where your medication comes from

Identity Aesthetics does not compound or dispense medication. When a licensed provider prescribes a medicine after an evaluation, the prescription is sent to a state-licensed pharmacy. This page lists the pharmacies we send prescriptions to, what each one is used for, and how to verify them yourself.

What compounded means

Compounded medicines are not FDA-approved

Compounded medicines are prepared by a licensed pharmacy for one patient at a time, based on a prescription. The FDA does not review compounded drugs for safety, effectiveness or quality before they are dispensed, the way it reviews approved drugs. Our providers prescribe a compounded medicine only when they document a medical reason that an FDA-approved product is not appropriate for that patient.

Compounded peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, MOTS-c, Epitalon, KPV and Semax) are prescribed only under the HHS enforcement discretion policy published October 30, 2026: single-ingredient products, in the forms and routes the policy covers, from FDA-registered ingredient sources with a certificate of analysis, and only with a patient-specific prescription. Peptides not covered by that policy are not offered.

503A and 503B are different. A 503A pharmacy compounds for individual patients with a prescription. A 503B outsourcing facility is registered with the FDA. The pharmacies listed above are used as 503A pharmacies.

Due-diligence standard

How we evaluate a pharmacy

  • Legal identity and facility — the pharmacy name, dispensing address and license must match.
  • Licensure where the patient is — the pharmacy must be licensed to dispense or ship to the patient's state.
  • Prescription only — every medicine follows a provider evaluation and a valid, patient-specific prescription. Payment alone is never a prescription.
  • Ingredient sourcing — compounded products must use ingredients from FDA-registered sources with a certificate of analysis.
  • Regulatory record — we consider inspection findings, warning letters and recalls when choosing a pharmacy.
